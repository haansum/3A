Shirin Aguilar and Elif Varga shared a management stable until a contract dispute split it. After Laredo, Elif mailed all fourteen fighters a cream card with a gold horseshoe and spelled one "Shereen"; Shirin made it her ring handle. Tonight's purse is owed to Marco, front row.

*

The ten-second clapper had just gone. Shirin threw the overhand from Elif's right, the shot that missed twice, falling in above her eyeline. It landed on the hairline with the knuckle ridge the padding doesn't cover, and the head went over like a lid. Elif stood, already absent. Then nothing held. Her arms fell, her chin tucked toward her knees, and she tipped away from the ankles, no bend in it, as her own corner's bucket went over. The canvas gave a hollow note the cheap seats heard, gave, and gave her up. She lay face up, lids half shut, set. Her jaw chewed at nothing. A groan came out, not a word.

*

I waved it, 2:52 of the fourth, and knelt. "Elif," I said at her ear. "Elif." The canvas smelled of chlorine.

The announcer started reading the result. Shirin stood over her, gloves on hips, and talked under him toward the bleachers by the swept dirt, empty where the Varga people had stood.

"Did any of them stay for the walkout, or just for yours?"

I moved my hand past Elif's face. Her eyes came after it late. Her cornerman held the stool upside down. A phone light in row three stayed on her.

"You saw that right hand late, didn't you? Did you read the card before you sealed it, or after?"

"I said sorry," Elif said.

"Winner by knockout, Shereen Aguilar."

Her cutman passed a cream card through the ropes. Shirin leaned over the top rope.

"Is that us square, Marco, or do I owe you for the stamp?"

She tucked the card under her glove strap and kept it.
