Soledad "Kindly" Mwangi is married to the man who used to work Ines Delacroix's corner, and last year Ines pulled her daughter Pia out of Soledad's Tuesday kids' class without a word. Ines, "The Valet," parked casino cars before this contract, and the contract holds her visa.

*

Arturo Benn watches the referee watch Ines's lead foot, so the uppercut starts outside all three of them and rises between her forearms. It lands high on the forehead. The head goes back; the hips stay loaded for a kick that never comes. The referee takes one step. She tips toward her right, kneels without meaning to, and sits into a chair that isn't there, legs locking straight like rails. Her raised hands shake. She ends face up, one bounce, then nothing, holding a guard nobody would choose. She stares at nothing. In, in, out. A blink, then much later another. Soledad lands two more before the referee gets there. The timekeeper's hammer comes down late.

*

The announcer reads round two, one forty-seven, then corrects it: forty-seven seconds. Over Arturo's table a fluorescent tube stutters. The referee holds Ines's mouthguard and pats a shirt with no pocket. Ines's cornerman, through the ropes before the wave-off, kneels with the towel still on his shoulder. Ines comes round wiping her mouth with her glove, again, again.

In the second row a girl asks, "Why did the man wait?"

Ninety seconds later Soledad is half out through the ropes, one foot on the apron, microphone in hand. She breathes out through her nose, once, long, and turns back to the referee.

"And thank you to her corner for the towel. Beautiful towel. Never left his shoulder."

The cornerman puts a hand on it.

"And thank you, sir, for arriving. Two punches. I counted for you."

The crowd howls.

"Hands up, eyes front, flat on her back with a perfect guard. I taught that to Pia at four-fifteen on Tuesdays. Nice to see somebody in the family kept it."

Phones go up in rows.

Ines, into her glove: "Pia's fine."

"Her gi's still on peg nine. I wash it Sundays." Soledad brings the other foot back in. "Tell Pia her spot's still hers. Free for any kid whose mom just learned to stay down."

Ines's corner says nothing. The cornerman folds the towel and puts it back on his shoulder.
