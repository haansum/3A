Had she really just asked the front row what the weather was like at the beach? Pell pressed the ice bag to Henrike's neck and it sweated through the towel onto his wrist. Across the ring Hadley Ishikawa was up off her stool early, working the near seats, telling them the replacement had come a long way to lose a lease. The seats liked her. They had liked her all night.

Pell had held pads for both of them under the same coach, and he had stood at that coach's memorial counting who came. Hadley had not. There was a photograph from that afternoon, sand and a paper umbrella, and Henrike had brought it up at the glove touch, low, lips hardly moving, so only Hadley and the referee got it. Hadley's mother had signed the ruling that cost Henrike a year; Henrike had mentioned that too.

Now Hadley cupped her hands and called over the old gym joke, the one about Coach and the borrowed generator that ran the lights for one round exactly. Henrike snorted. So did Hadley. Both of them looked away at once, furious, like people caught praying.

Pell took the bag off. Out behind the bleachers a real generator droned. Round three would go to the funny one. He already disliked knowing it.

The bell.
